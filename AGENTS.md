# Jarcade

- Build a collection of simple games in Rust with Macroquad, sharing game and UI code across iOS, Android, PC, and web.
- Put games on a minimal home screen, grouped by category (single player, multiplayer, etc.), with easy access to app settings.
- Avoid needless redraws and background work; keep game timing independent of rendering. Power saver is OFF by default; enabling it uses a true black, essential-only UI and fewer updates.
- Use a polished, minimal WHITE default theme, sharp high-DPI graphics, generous touch targets, and keyboard controls. Never trade away phone resolution by default. Normal gameplay follows the display refresh rate without an FPS cap; keep motion and turns smooth. The optional FPS counter must not force idle screens to redraw.
- Game cards must show an actual gameplay preview; use two columns on phones and more on larger screens. Add subtle, optional haptics on supported devices and clearly handle unsupported browsers.
- Minesweeper offers board sizes before play, large scrollable/zoomable tiles using most of the screen, and a fixed, clear Reveal/Flag selector. Drags and pinch gestures must never reveal or flag tiles.
- Fih is a detailed, round, cute front-facing fish drawn and animated in Rust code in fullscreen hand-authored SVG rooms with clean, flat furniture: kitchen, bathroom, bedroom, playroom, and clinic. Use in-scene icons, room-specific care (drag food to the mouth with gaze/chewing; rub soap before rinsing), idle/action animations, compact icon stats, varied food with saved pantry stock, and clothing/hat previews before purchase. Preserve offline progress; mini-games live inside Fih, never on the arcade launch screen.
- Fih controls belong to their rooms: wardrobe only in the bedroom, potion cabinet/shop in the clinic, throwable ball and progressively harder mini-games in the playroom. Keep pantry and shop distinct, track held fingers, show needs and food refusal through expressions, place soap bubbles along strokes, and center coins/level. App settings belong only on the launch screen; adapt every screen to phone, tablet, and desktop sizes.
- Keep game rules separate from rendering and test gameplay, edge cases, and regressions. Run formatting, linting, tests, and relevant builds before finishing changes; state platform verification limits honestly.

- Coupe and Dicksit are online room-code multiplayer games with original art. Keep rules in `src/multiplayer`, validate moves on the room server, never expose other hands or unrevealed votes, preserve reconnect seats, and leave static multiplayer screens event-driven. Dicksit uses the 84 generated illustrations in `assets/reverie`; Fih stays SVG/code.
- Keep multiplayer playful and minimal: Coupe is a woodland card table with coin chips and role-colored actions; Dicksit is a lilac dream gallery with framed pictures and a clear clue. Avoid generic form-like game screens and decorative clutter.

- Wavelength is offline multiplayer on one device: private target peek, concealed handoff, draggable guessing dial, then an unscored reveal. Preserve the reference flow, prompt decks/custom spectra, directly editable sides, high-DPI vectors, and event-driven idle screens; use a warm cream Wavelength background and true black in power saver; never open a room connection.

- Table tennis is offline single player against a bounded-speed computer: finger/mouse rackets, legal net-crossing shots and bounces, three difficulties, first to 11 with a two-point lead, and alternating service. Keep physics independent of refresh rate, pause on interruptions, and rotate the court on short landscape screens.

- Wolvesville uses private online rooms for 6–16 friends, Classic/Advanced/custom role lists, original vector portraits, private roles/findings/pack/ghost chats, and server-timed nights, discussion and secret majority votes. Validate setups and advanced powers on the server, preserve reconnect seats, and redraw static matches only for events or the one-second countdown.

- Codenames uses online red/blue teams, private spymaster keys, English/Portuguese word boards, and server-validated clues and guesses. Never send unrevealed identities to operatives; preserve team seats on reconnect.
- Nonograms is offline single player with original, uniquely solvable picture puzzles and endless generated logical puzzles, board sizes, Fill/Cross/Move tools, touch painting, undo/hints, zoom/pan, and saved progress. Space switches Fill/Cross; marked cells must be cleared before placing the opposite mark, including drags and hints. Use a white, ink-and-coral pixel-art UI, compact icon controls with accessible labels/visual help, and real progress thumbnails. Keep the board and launch gallery usable on small screens without idle redraws.

- Sudoku is offline with endlessly generated Classic, Killer, XV, Kropki, Thermo and Diagonal puzzles at Easy/Medium/Hard levels. Accept a generated puzzle only after a variant-aware logical proof of its unique solution. Hard targets expert, long-session solves: require a measured advanced-logic floor (45 cells stalled under the old techniques, 5 advanced deductions, 3 forcing nets, a 30-placement implication branch); reject easy/unproved candidates, keep generation cancellable, and preserve existing saves. Keep rules/generation separate from rendering; use a clean SudokuPad-inspired board and number pad, corner/centre notes, colours, multi-cell selection, undo/redo, explanatory hints, check, zoom/pan and saved progress without idle redraws. Flag incorrect Sudoku digits immediately; allow deselection, and use number input with no selected cells to highlight matching revealed digits and, in a lighter shade, corner/centre pencil-note possibilities without editing progress. Repeat cell taps deselect; quick double-taps fill a single distinct user-noted candidate, never a hidden answer. Space toggles only Digit/Corner notes; preserve drag selection and pinch/pan without accidental entries. An icon-only magic wand (N) fills initial empty-cell notes only before the first progress edit; hide it permanently after use or an edit, including across undo/reset/reload, and restore it only for a new puzzle. It fills notes with only classic row/column/box candidates from visible digits, ignoring variant rules, hidden answers and deductions; never auto-fill a digit, and save it as one undoable action. Large digit entries (including double-taps and hints) remove that digit from corner/centre notes in classic row/column/box peers; group the cleanup with the entry for undo/redo, without extra logic or automatic note restoration.
